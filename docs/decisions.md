# Decision index

Settled 2026-11-05 after a prior-art study and two independent design
reviews. [`prior-art.md`](./prior-art.md) holds the survey, the rejected
alternatives and the evidence behind most rows.

Reversing or narrowing a row needs a later row that names the row it
refines or supersedes, or an ADR in `docs/adr/` when the reasons need more
than a row. Mark the earlier row's status with the row or ADR that replaces
it; never rewrite a row in place.

| 95  | Tombstones for deleted comments (refines 55 and 65, which said no tombstones). **Shape.** A tombstone is `{ id, deleted }`: the comment's id and the time of the deletion, no text. The sidecar, the link payload and the IndexedDB record carry an optional `deleted` list beside `comments`. **Rules.** A deletion beats a copy of the comment whose `updated` is not after it; an edit made after the deletion wins and the tombstone is dropped. Deleting stamps `max(now, updated + 1)`. Tombstones merge by id and keep the later time. A document keeps at most 500 tombstones (the comment cap); past that the oldest go, so a deletion older than the 500 newer ones can be undone by a very old file. That is accepted. **Compatibility.** `version` stays 1. `deleted` is optional and Valibot's object schema drops unknown keys, so a build without this row reads new files and links as before and ignores the tombstones. Files and links from before this row have no `deleted` and read as an empty list. A build without this row that saves a record drops its tombstones; the next merge with a newer copy brings them back. No `NEWER_VERSION` notice is involved. **Stored time (refines 65).** A time more than 5 minutes ahead of the clock is not trusted: `updated` falls back to `created` when that is sane, otherwise 0, and a tombstone's time falls back to 0. A time up to 5 minutes ahead is kept as written. The old rule clamped to now at parse time, so a forged or skewed copy won every merge, and won again on every re-import. **Saves.** The store merges what it is given with the stored record inside one IndexedDB transaction, so a second tab that saves a stale list neither overwrites the first tab's comments nor brings back its deletions. A click on a link, or one that ends a text selection, opens nothing, and an image inside a link keeps the link. | 88  | Mirroring the presenter's laser, marks and diagram focus (refines 8, 29, 68 and 73; narrows the out-of-scope clause of 87). **Direction and content.** Outward from the presenter only: to followers through the room, and from the audience window to the presenter window through the BroadcastChannel bridge. Mirrored: the laser (the audience window's own, and the phone remote's, which the presenter relays), pen and arrow strokes, erasing, undo, redo and clear, a change of slide, and the diagram focus view (open, close, zoom and pan), which goes to followers only, because the presenter window shows scaled, inert copies of the slides. Nothing travels back from a follower or from the presenter window. Not mirrored: the code, table and image views and their line marks (87; they stay on the roadmap), sketches, the chosen tool and colour. Marks stay in memory (29); saving them is a separate roadmap item. **Threat model.** (1) The relay and the network see ciphertext only and can drop, delay, reorder or repeat frames; AES-GCM and the signature stop them from reading or forging, and the replay guard (68) drops a repeated counter. (2) A follower holds the view key, so it can read every state and mirror frame and can seal any message under that key. It cannot sign: a follower takes a `state` or `mirror` frame only when the presenter's ECDSA signature (73) verifies, and the presenter takes nothing under the view key but a follower's hello. A follower therefore cannot place a mark, a laser or a focus view on another follower, and cannot move the presenter. (3) A phone remote or agent holds the control key and still cannot sign. Mirror frames are sealed under the view key only, so remotes and agents never receive them, and the phone's laser reaches followers only as the presenter's own signed `laser` op. (4) A hostile message that is signed (a hostile presenter, a hostile same-origin page on the bridge) is still bounded: every op is a `v.strictObject` of numbers, picklists and a slide address; no string from a message reaches the DOM except an address compared for equality; marks are SVG paths that docwalk writes from the numbers with `setAttribute`; a colour is one of three keys mapped to design tokens on the receiving page; the focus op names a diagram by its number on the slide, and the view shows the receiver's own sanitized diagram. (5) Not covered: another follower can repeat a frame the presenter signed to a late joiner that has not seen its counter, which shows a stale mark, laser or focus view until the presenter's next one (as for 73); the relay can drop a `focus-close` or `laser-off` (the laser hides itself after 3 s, a focus view closes with Escape or the next resync); a follower whose pagination differs from the presenter's shows marks only on a page with the presenter's address, never on the fallback page. **Message.** One new `RemoteMessage`, `mirror`, with an `op`: `laser` (x, y in 0..1), `laser-off`, `stroke` (slide address, id, tool `pen` or `arrow`, colour `primary`, `focus` or `ink`, `from`, `points`, `end`), `drop` (ids), `clear`, `focus-open` (slide address and the diagram's number on that page), `focus-view` (scale 0.1 to 12; pan as a fraction of the stage, so a bigger screen shows the same framing) and `focus-close`. The presenter signs every `mirror` with the key of 73, inside the ciphertext, so the relay's frame schema and 16 KB cap (`worker/room.ts`) do not change. The follower accepts `mirror` only from a signed frame, in the same place as `state`. **Coordinates.** Slide units, not pixels: x over the width and y over the height of the 1280 by 720 slide box, on a 4096 grid (0.3 px), clamped to the box. The receiver draws into an SVG with the same 1280 by 720 view box inside the slide box that reveal.js scales, so marks sit on the same spot at any window size, in the card or the fill frame (77), and in the presenter window's preview. A stroke carries its slide address (21) and appears only while the receiver shows that address. **Load.** The laser keeps the 33 ms throttle (about 30 messages a second). A pen stroke records a point only when it is 2 slide px from the last (a 600 px line is about 300 points), stops at 1500 points, and goes out in chunks of at most 128 points every 80 ms while it is drawn (12 frames a second at most, about 1.5 KB each) with `end` on the last; the receiver appends a chunk only when `from` equals the points it holds. An arrow goes out once, on release. Receivers keep at most 16 slides, 200 strokes and 30,000 points per slide, and 1500 per stroke; anything above is ignored. **Erase, undo, redo, slide change.** The layer reports the marks that are visible after every change that is not a stroke in progress. The sender compares them with what it sent for that slide: a stroke that appears is sent in full (redo and an undone erase send the stroke again), one that vanished goes out as `drop`, an empty slide as `clear`. A change of slide sends `clear` and the new slide's strokes, so a follower that missed an erase on that slide converges. **Late join and reconnect.** The follower's `hello` already makes the presenter send `state`. It now also schedules one resync, at least 150 ms later and at least 2 s after the last: `clear`, every visible stroke of the current slide (and the one in progress) and the focus state. It is a broadcast of idempotent ops (a stroke with `from` 0 replaces its id), so a hostile follower that says hello often gets one resync per 2 s from the presenter, and followers that already have the marks lose nothing. It is separate from `state` and from the document link, which stay the size they were. The presenter also resyncs when its own socket reopens. The presenter window asks for the same resync once, with its first hello; the heartbeat hello does not. **Static mode.** The remote controls stay hidden without `/api` (rule 6). The presenter window mirror works there, because it uses the BroadcastChannel only. A mirror op sent to the room is ciphertext inside the frames the room already uses; nothing besides the document leaves the browser (rule 8). **Rejected.** Sending SVG or path text (hostile markup, and larger than the points); putting marks in `state` (it is signed and sent twice on every slide change and every hello); addressed parts for the late joiner (followers would need a part assembler for view-key frames, which a view-key holder could flood); a CRDT (one writer); sending the pen's raw pointer stream (hundreds of events a second, more than the frame budget). | accepted |
